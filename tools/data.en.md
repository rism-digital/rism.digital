### Data management

The core duty of the RISM project is to create and curate a rich set of data about music sources, with currently about 1.5 million records. The underlying format used by RISM is [MarcXML](https://www.loc.gov/standards/marcxml//), the most widely used bibliographic format. Music source descriptions in RISM include encoded music notation incipits, with currently 2.25 million incipits in the full RISM dataset. Incipits are encoded in [MEI](https://music-encoding.org/) format. Increasingly, records describing a source are accompanied by digitized images. These are made accessible through the International Image Interoperability Framework ([IIIF](https://iiif.io/)). 
All bibliographical and musical data produced by RISM are public and free to use and reuse. As a rule, digitizations of sources are open too. The RISM Digital Center manages the huge quantity of data produced by the RISM worldwide, following the FAIR principles for scientific data management: Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. This qualifies the RISM to be registered in [re3data](http://re3data.org/repository/r3d100013190), an international reference for open data repositories.
